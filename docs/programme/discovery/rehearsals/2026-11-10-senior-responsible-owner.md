# Rehearsal: Senior Responsible Owner

Interviewer: Innovox (rehearsal) · Date: 10 November 2026 · Topic: default script and platform concept

**Walk me through the last time you had to make a decision based on security evidence.**
I don't read the evidence myself. I'm accountable for cyber security outcomes across the capability, and I set the risk we're prepared to accept within the appetite delegated to me. What reaches me is a summary from my security lead: what the residual risks are, and whether they're inside appetite or need escalating.

**What took longest? What was redone?**
Getting a clear picture across all the suppliers at once. I tend to find out about problems late, when they're already affecting the timeline.

**What would you need to trust evidence produced by a tool?**
I'd need my security lead and the assessors to trust it. For me it's about whether my decisions are defensible later. If someone asks why I accepted a risk, I need to show what I knew and when.

**Who decides whether a tool like this is used, and how is it bought?**
I could set an expectation across the programme, and that would drive adoption by suppliers. But I'd only do it on my security lead's advice, and it would go through commercial.

**What would make you stop using it?**
If it became a dependency that added risk of its own. If the tool itself sat outside the sovereignty requirements we apply to everything else, I couldn't use it.

**Concept: the platform generates evidence continuously, records who approved what and when, and assesses its own sovereignty.**
The record of decisions is what interests me: who accepted which risk, on what evidence, and when. That makes my decisions defensible. Earlier warning across suppliers would be valuable. I'd want proof the tool meets the same standards it checks, and I'd want to know what happens to our data if the supplier of the tool goes away.

## Objections raised
- Does this add a dependency or a new risk?
- What is the sovereignty of the tool itself?
- What happens to our data if the tool's supplier goes away?

## Assumptions this rehearsal suggests
- SROs value a defensible decision record more than the evidence itself.
- An SRO-level expectation across a programme would drive supplier adoption.
- Proving the platform meets its own standards is a precondition for SRO support.
- Exit and data-return terms matter at SRO level, not only to commercial.
