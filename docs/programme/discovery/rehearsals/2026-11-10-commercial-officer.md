# Rehearsal: commercial officer

Interviewer: Innovox (rehearsal) · Date: 10 November 2026 · Topic: default script and platform concept

**Walk me through the last time security evidence came up in your work.**
My part is putting the contract terms in place that enforce security through the lifecycle: the security conditions, the Def Stan obligations where they apply, what the supplier must provide and when. I don't assess the evidence, but I get involved when a supplier isn't providing what the contract requires.

**What took longest? What was redone?**
Disputes about what the contract actually required. If the evidence obligation is vague, the supplier and the delivery team read it differently.

**What would you need to trust a tool like this?**
That there's a compliant route to buy it, clear pricing, and clear terms on data: who owns the evidence, where it's held, and how we get it back if we stop.

**Who decides whether a tool like this is used, and how is it bought?**
The requirement comes from the delivery team and security. I find the route: a framework such as G-Cloud, a marketplace purchase if our cloud agreement allows it, or a procurement. If you're not on a route we can use, it gets much harder.

**What would make you stop using it?**
Lock-in. If the evidence only exists inside your product in a format nobody else can read, that's a problem at contract end.

**Concept: delivered via G-Cloud, cloud marketplaces or customer tenancy, with evidence in open formats (OSCAL, SARIF, CycloneDX).**
Open formats help a lot with the exit question. Customer tenancy helps with data. If we wanted suppliers to use it, I'd need wording for a contract clause, and I'd need to know whether we pay or the supplier does.

## Objections raised
- Is there a compliant route to buy this?
- Who owns the evidence data?
- Lock-in at contract end

## Assumptions this rehearsal suggests
- A G-Cloud listing is a precondition for government buyers, not an extra.
- Open evidence formats reduce commercial objections to lock-in.
- Ready-made contract wording would make a mandate easier.
- It is unresolved whether the buyer or the supplier pays when a buyer mandates use.

## Questions to ask real people
- Which routes to market can you use for a tool like this, and which are fastest?
- What exit and data-return terms do you require for tools holding evidence?
- If suppliers were required to use a tool, who would normally pay?
